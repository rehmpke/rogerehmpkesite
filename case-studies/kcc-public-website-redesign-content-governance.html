KCC Public Website & Content Governance

Kankakee Community College
Public web architecture / static-site migration / content governance / publishing efficiency

My contribution Key decision Results Academic Programs & AI search

My contribution and delivery

I helped lead KCC’s public website redesign and transition from departmental SharePoint publishing to a centralized, standards-based model. The redesign began with an outside consultant and our internal web team; its 2018 planning scope covered more than 1,200 pages across the public website and related sites.

When COVID disrupted normal operations and consultant support ended, I took responsibility for the remaining work alongside our web developer. Working remotely, we completed the redesign, migration, and related site and subsite work, launching the architecture that continues to support kcc.edu.

Problem

Departments previously authored and published public website content directly in SharePoint. Web Services routinely corrected inconsistent formatting and page structures after publication, while ITS supported the underlying technical environment. The redesign needed to address both the aging platform and the publishing process.

Key constraints

  • The redesign and migration were already underway with consultant involvement.
  • COVID disrupted normal collaboration and project continuity.
  • External consultant support ended before the project was complete.
  • The main site and related sites/subsites still needed to be migrated into the new model.
  • The internal web team needed to support the architecture long-term.
  • Remote coordination became the working model.

Architecture and publishing decision

Jekyll generates the public site, CloudCannon handles editing, builds, hosting, and publishing, and GitHub holds the source code and version history. Structured content workflows, including Google Sheets-driven patterns, support repeatable publishing. This gave the web team greater control over templates, content structure, and ongoing development.

Our web developer and I manage site updates with a small group of Marketing editors and authors. Departments continue to own their information and request changes through Web Services. This allows us to review content, page structure, and accessibility before publication rather than routinely correct problems afterward.

Results

Centralized publishing reduced recurring corrective work and shifted day-to-day editing, builds, and releases primarily to Web Services, with less routine reliance on ITS. The low-cost architecture remains in use and supports ongoing development.

Academic Programs: launch, review, and refinement

Student discovery

The same operating model supports ongoing improvements to how students discover academic programs. I helped shape that work with our web developer, Marketing, academic departments, and other stakeholders. We moved beyond catalog-only information toward program pages with career context, videos, clearer calls to action, and Lightcast labor market data. The goal was to help students understand their options, career opportunities, and academic pathways.

Launch and review

The redesigned Academic Programs page launched in August 2025 with updated navigation and a pathway-based structure. During the launch-period review, college leadership agreed the structure was a significant improvement and should remain intact. We identified content and discoverability as the next priorities: clearer pathway terminology, introductions aligned with student goals, stronger metadata and internal links, and structured data where appropriate.

Next investment

Our web developer took on the follow-up content and search enhancements, with continued executive sponsorship. We agreed to monitor engagement, search performance, and how effectively the content guided students toward next steps. The review gave the team a focused improvement plan while preserving the completed structural work. By July 2026, the broader program-page effort included expanded schema and further search improvements, with additional content still being refined.

Search and AI discovery

This work connects traditional SEO with Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO): improving how program information can be found and understood through search engines and AI-generated answers. My contribution brings student-focused content strategy together with metadata, structured data, and ongoing review of discoverability.

Strategic value

Beyond the redesign, KCC gained a sustainable public web operation that continues to support accessibility, analytics, content standards, search visibility, and AI discoverability.
